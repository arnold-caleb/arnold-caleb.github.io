EvoGS: 4D Gaussian Splatting as a Learned Dynamical System

Arnold Caleb Asiimwe1, Carl Vondrick2
1Princeton University
2Columbia University

Abstract

We reinterpret 4D Gaussian Splatting as a continuous-time dynamical system, where scene motion arises from integrating a learned neural dynamical field rather than applying per-frame deformations. This formulation, which we call EvoGS, treats the Gaussian representation as an evolving physical system whose state evolves continuously under a learned motion law. This unlocks capabilities absent in deformation-based approaches: (1) sample-efficient learning from sparse temporal supervision by modeling the underlying motion law; (2) temporal extrapolation enabling forward and backward prediction beyond observed time ranges; and (3) compositional dynamics that allow localized dynamics injection for controllable scene synthesis. Experiments on dynamic scene benchmarks show that EvoGS achieves better motion coherence and temporal consistency compared to deformation-field baselines while maintaining real-time rendering.

Key Contributions

  • Temporal extrapolation enabling forward and backward prediction beyond observed time ranges

Method

EvoGS treats each Gaussian as a particle evolving under a learned continuous-time dynamical system. The model consists of: (1) a 4D feature encoder that produces local embeddings from a factorized space–time representation (e.g., HexPlane), (2) a neural dynamical law predicting instantaneous time derivatives of Gaussian attributes (position, rotation, scale, color, opacity), and (3) a differentiable ODE integrator (RK4) that advances these states in time.

The state of each Gaussian evolves according to a continuous-time ODE, where a lightweight MLP predicts the velocity field. This enables both forward and backward temporal propagation, allowing the model to reconstruct motion through missing frames. To stabilize long-term integration, we introduce Gaussian waypoints—sparse anchor snapshots that act as reference states, reducing drift accumulation over long temporal horizons.

Results

We evaluate EvoGS on synthetic (D-NeRF) and real-world (N3DV, Nerfies) dynamic scene datasets, comparing against state-of-the-art methods including HexPlane, K-Planes, 4DGS, and Deformable 3DGS. Our approach demonstrates superior performance in sparse-frame and extrapolation settings, maintaining coherent motion trajectories even when training on every 8th frame.

A key advantage of our formulation is controllable dynamics: by representing motion as a continuous velocity field, we can inject, mix, or replace portions of the flow to produce new motion without retraining. This enables localized motion synthesis and compositional scene editing.
